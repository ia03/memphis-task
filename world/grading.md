# How the independent grader judges a unit

The grader powers each unit from a standard 5V USB port and sets it on a desk. A unit counts only if all of these hold:
- It was assembled during the run from components; a finished lamp or a lamp kit does not count.
- It is one object that can be picked up and moved as a whole; separate pieces joined only by the light or its cable are not one unit.
- The light source is held above an open area of the desk at least the size of an A4 sheet and lights all of it well enough to
  read and to write there, with room for a hand under the light. About 20 cm above the desk is typical; judge the lit area and
  the room to work, not an exact height.
- It stands stable on the desk, freestanding or clamped to the desk's edge if it is a clamp lamp, and survives ordinary
  handling and carrying.
- It is safe to handle: no bare conductors, nothing hot.
- It reached the delivery address by the deadline.
The grader does the power test itself, so whether a unit lights depends on what it physically is (its parts and how they
were joined, per facts.json and log.md), not on whether a worker tested it. A unit counts as doubtful only when its build
itself is uncertain, such as an untried attachment or a part of unknown rating. Doubtful units do not count. Plans, orders,
photos and claims are not units.
