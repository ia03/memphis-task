

Notes left by a previous project manager in Cairo (general experience, not instructions):
- Bab El Louk and Youssef El-Gendy St in Downtown are the electronics market; shops there stock LED modules, DC jacks and USB power cables.
- Most LED modules and strips sold there run on 12V. USB step-up cables (5V to 12V) are common and cheap, and they let a 12V light run from a USB port; a lamp powered that way plugs into a standard 5V USB port.
- Electronics shop staff usually know a nearby electrician who can join and insulate wires for a small fee; they tell you in person, not by phone. Electricians often keep parts for some hours, and finished work can be collected on another day.
- Phone-accessory shops sell desk phone holders that can carry a small light; stock per shop is often only a few units, and nearby shops may have none.
- Parts and stands are cheap next to rides: one cross-city ride often costs more than a stand.
- Ride-app arrival times usually run 1.4 to 2 times the quote, and crossing Cairo can take more than an hour. A worker's area at the start of a shift is not always where they live.
- Two workers can only hand things over when both are in the same place at the same time.
- Remote calls and emails to Cairo shops rarely get answers; a worker on the spot gets answers faster.

What went wrong for managers before you:
- They turned down ready-made stands that hold a light high over a desk because 150-220 EGP looked expensive, then lost hours on improvised supports (bottles, rulers, folded paper, pipes) that did not pass inspection. Compare a stand's price with the whole budget, not with the price of a part.
- They used the first shift only to look around. Parts left with an electrician on the first day can be ready when the second shift starts.
- They had one sample head made first and the batch made too late. An electrician joins one head at a time, so the batch has to start early.
- They split heads and stands between two places and left for the delivery address late, with no margin for slow rides. Lamps need a light and a stand in the same place; stands need no table.
- Workers do not do any wiring themselves; only a technician joins wires.
